# The Grandson's Visit (1532 CE)

The pustules had begun to weep. Nayra felt them like small fires across his chest and face, each one a coal pressed into flesh. He lay on woven blankets in the corner of his stone house, watching light shift through the doorway. Morning or afternoon—he could no longer tell.

Footsteps on the path outside. Quick, young, familiar.

His grandson's face appeared in the doorway, silhouetted against brightness. The boy froze.

"Grandfather."

"Come." Nayra's voice came out broken, a sound like dry reeds. "I won't shatter."

The young man crossed the threshold slowly, as if entering a tomb. He had grown since the last visit—shoulders broader, jaw more defined. A chasqui's legs, strong from running the empire's roads. But his eyes were still a child's eyes, wide now with what he saw: the lesions mapping his grandfather's skin, the sunken cheeks, the smell of fever-sweat and something worse beneath it.

"I came as soon as I heard." He knelt beside the blankets. "The messages I carried—I knew what this sickness does. But I didn't think—"

"Sit with me." Nayra reached out, and his grandson took his hand despite the pustules. Warm grip. Strong. "Tell me of your runs. I want to hear."

---

The room softened. Nayra felt himself sliding backward, five years perhaps, to when his body still answered him and his grandson had burst through this same doorway with news burning on his tongue.

*The boy was younger then, barely tested on the mountain roads, but already proud of the quipu knots he'd learned to read. Nayra had been repairing sandals by the fire.*

*"Grandfather! Strange men have been seen in Tumbes. Men with hair on their faces, riding beasts like giant llamas but faster, with hooves that ring against stone."*

*Nayra had set down his work. "Who speaks of this?"*

*"Everyone on the northern routes. And there is more." The boy's excitement flickered, became something else. "The Sapa Inca Huayna Capac is dead. A sickness—pustules that cover the skin and burn with fever. His chosen heir Ninan Cuyuchi died days later from the same affliction."*

*The fire crackled. Outside, wind moved across the altiplano.*

*Nayra had looked at his grandson—this boy following in his footsteps, carrying words across the empire's body—and felt pride swell in his chest. But beneath it, something cold had stirred. A disease that could kill a living god. Strange men on unknown beasts. The pattern of an ending, though he could not yet read it.*

*"You did well to bring this news," he had said. "Rest now. Eat."*

---

The memory dissolved. His grandson was speaking—something about Atahualpa, about victory—but the words scattered like startled birds.

**The room folded.**

**Two boys scrambled over the great stones of Akapana, laughing, shoving each other. Royal garments. An imperial pilgrimage. Brothers—he knew them somehow, though he had never seen their faces. Huáscar. Atahualpa. Playing on ruins older than their bloodline, unaware of the war coiled in their future.**

**The image shuddered, slipped—**

**Two girls on the same stones. Different clothes, strange colors. The pyramid partially restored, ropes marking pathways he did not recognize. They poked at the ground with sticks, bored and restless, while adults worked nearby with tools he could not name. The same sacred ground, the same sky above it, but the air tasted of a distant century.**

**Then nothing.**

Nayra gasped. His grandson's face swam back into focus, creased with concern.

"Grandfather? You went somewhere."

Nayra's heart pounded against his ribs. The two boys. The two girls. The same stones holding all of them, separated by—what? His fever-addled mind grasped at the vision's edges, but it was already fading, leaving only the sense that time had become unreliable. That the walls between moments were thinning.

"I am here," he managed.

But he was not certain that was true anymore.
